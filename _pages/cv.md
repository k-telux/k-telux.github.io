  <section class="cv-section" aria-labelledby="research-experience">
    <div class="cv-section-heading"><h2 id="research-experience">Research in practice.</h2><p>From single-photon emission to the control of individual atoms.</p></div>
    <div class="cv-research-list">
      <article class="cv-research-card" aria-labelledby="single-photon-emitter-creation-in-few-layer-2d-materials">
        <header><p class="cv-date">2024 · Rice University</p><h3 id="single-photon-emitter-creation-in-few-layer-2d-materials">Single-photon emitter creation in few-layer 2D materials</h3></header>
        <p class="cv-mentors">Supervisors: <a href="https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=lm68m7kAAAAJ&amp;hl=en">Wenjing Wu</a> and <a href="https://profiles.rice.edu/faculty/shengxi-huang">Prof. Shengxi Huang</a><br><a href="https://scopelab.rice.edu/">SCOPE Lab</a></p>
        <ul>
          <li>Carried out optical spectroscopy measurements including photoluminescence (PL), Raman, and time-correlated single-photon counting (TCSPC) on hBN-encapsulated WSe<sub>2</sub> and WS<sub>2</sub> monolayers integrated with strain-engineered substrates.</li>
          <li>Examined single-photon emission quality through photon-statistics measurements, obtaining the second-order correlation function <i>g</i><sup>(2)</sup>(τ) using Hanbury Brown and Twiss (HBT) interferometry.</li>
        </ul>
      </article>
      <article class="cv-research-card" aria-labelledby="single-atom-trapping-technique-based-on-movable-optical-lattices">
        <header><p class="cv-date">2022 — 2024 · USTC</p><h3 id="single-atom-trapping-technique-based-on-movable-optical-lattices">Single-atom trapping with movable optical lattices</h3></header>
        <p>This program was proposed to trap single atoms and combine fluorescence detection with movable optical lattices for high-precision control of atomic positions.</p>
        <p class="cv-mentors">Supervisors: <a href="https://faculty.ustc.edu.cn/wangjian1">Dr. Jian Wang</a> and <a href="http://lqcc.ustc.edu.cn/cfli/">Prof. Chuanfeng Li</a><br><a href="https://lqcc.ustc.edu.cn/">CAS Key Laboratory of Quantum Information</a></p>
        <ul>
          <li>Participated in setting up the optical path in a magneto-optical trap (MOT) for <sup>87</sup>Rb. Independently built the double-pass configuration to adjust the frequency of cooling light with Bragg diffraction.</li>
          <li>Led the building and testing of the second ultrahigh-vacuum system with optical access, mounting an Rb atom dispenser for the MOT and optical dipole traps for ensembles and single atoms. The system reached 3 × 10<sup>−11</sup> Torr.</li>
          <li>Independently pre-treated the optical fiber for use in the vacuum system and tested the mode-field diameter of the treated single-mode fiber to optimize mode matching between the cavity and fiber.</li>
          <li>Independently designed control circuits and installed control electronics—including the microwave amplifier, radio-frequency switch, and radio-frequency generator—into integrated chassis to facilitate connections between electrical devices in the optical path.</li>
          <li>Achieved atom control for 3 seconds <span class="cv-unpublished">(unpublished)</span>.</li>
        </ul>
      </article>
